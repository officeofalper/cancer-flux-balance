Why Burn Glucose to Lactate?

A cancer cell can turn glucose into ATP fast but wastefully (aerobic glycolysis), or slowly but efficiently (mitochondria). Room inside the cell is limited. Change the glucose supply, oxygen and the space cost of mitochondria, and watch which route the best-growing cell would choose.

Try these experiments

  1. Find the switch. Start at "Low glucose" and raise the glucose capacity slowly. Below 10 all glucose is burned in mitochondria. Above 10 the mitochondria are full, and every extra glucose goes to lactate.
  2. Yield vs rate. Watch "ATP per glucose" fall while "ATP made per time" still rises. The cell wastes glucose but makes ATP faster.
  3. Make mitochondria cheap. Lower the space cost below 16 (that is 32 ÷ 2). The Warburg effect disappears: the cell never makes lactate, however much glucose it has.
  4. Take away oxygen. Lower oxygen to 30 or less. The switch moves to lower glucose, because now oxygen, not space, limits the mitochondria.

How the model works

Two routes, two unknown fluxes: aerobic glycolysis JAG (glucose → 2 lactate, 2 ATP) and the oxidative route JOX (glucose → 6 CO₂, ~32 ATP). The cell picks the fluxes that make ATP fastest:

maximize   2·J_AG + 32·J_OX
subject to c_AG·J_AG + c_OX·J_OX ≤ 1   (space)
           J_AG + J_OX           ≤ G   (glucose)
           6·J_OX                ≤ O2  (oxygen)

This is a tiny flux balance analysis problem, solved by linear programming. cOX = 0.1; cAG = 0.1 ÷ (space cost ratio). Glycolysis wins at high glucose only if it makes more ATP per unit of space, i.e. if the ratio is above 32 ÷ 2 = 16.

Where this comes from

Based on my published review "Metabolic Reprogramming in Cancer Cells" (#94), which describes the Warburg effect: tumor cells convert much of their glucose to lactate even when oxygen is available, while their mitochondria keep working.

The model structure follows Vazquez et al. (2010, BMC Systems Biology 4:58), who explained aerobic glycolysis as a trade-off between ATP yield and the space (solvent capacity) that enzymes and mitochondria occupy.

All numbers are arbitrary and chosen to show behaviour. This model only looks at ATP; version 2 adds the building blocks a dividing cell needs.
